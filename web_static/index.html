Doppler flyover geolocation read-only preset viewer

About this viewer

This page rebuilds the method from colsto's video "I discovered a sound that leaks your exact location": the engine tone of a propeller aircraft flying past a microphone traces a Doppler curve in the spectrogram, and the shape and timing of that curve can be matched against a day of flight-tracking (ADS-B) data to find where the recording was made, even when no plane is visible.

  1. Fit each curve. Four parameters: base pitch f0, speed v, closest distance d and the time t0 at which the closest-approach sound arrives (with propagation delay, the "retarded" model). Curves are fitted with the sliders, by tracing the ridge, or found automatically (whitening, harmonic stacking, a template bank, Viterbi ridge tracing, least-squares fit).
  2. Gaps, not clock times. With two or more curves, the time between their t0s (920 s in the Oyster Bay example) is derived from the fits; every pair is checked, with a tolerance of offset_tol_s + k·√(σj²+σk²).
  3. Candidate spots. Flights are filtered to the fitted speed range, jets are dropped (ICAO DOC 8643 engine type), and a 200 m grid is laid inside each event's distance band. Each spot gets the sound-delayed closest-approach time of each flight, using terrain heights.
  4. Offset filter. A spot survives only if it would hear one flight per event at the observed gaps (batch nearest-neighbour search).
  5. Silence filter. Spots where another aircraft would have been audible between the events are dropped (two passes).
  6. Crude rank, densify to 100 m, locked spectrogram scoring. For each spot the predicted curve of each flight (emission-time model on the real track) is laid on the spectrogram; event 1 sweeps dt and f0, the others sweep f0 only with their times locked by geometry. The score is the mean whitened brightness under the curves.
  7. Grouping. The best spot per combination of flights is kept and ranked. Heat maps show the score on a 25 m grid around a match.
  8. Single-flight mode (one curve, known recording time): every flight segment at that time is scored by −mean |fmodel − ftraced| after a time and f0 sweep; best spot per flight.

This site is read-only. Everything here was precomputed by the full app and exported to static files. Uploading your own video/audio, auto-detecting or re-fitting curves, and running new searches need the full app (Python backend with about 30 GB of flight data), which is not hosted here. The f0/v/d/t0 sliders below only draw a curve in your browser.

Data: flight tracks from adsb.lol (ODbL), terrain from AWS Terrarium tiles, maps © OpenStreetMap contributors, imagery © Esri. Livermore recordings: published flyover dataset with known recorder position (derived from the tracks to ~2 m). Altenthann: freesound #847111 (audio only). FeederWatch: Cornell Lab live cam, recorded with live adsb.lol polling.
